What I changed and why

A short note on the redesign, written for the Qoruz design team.

What works today

The page already holds the right data: similar-creator benchmarks, an AI summary, a full audience breakdown and brand history. Add to list is the primary button.

What doesn't

It answers "what are her numbers?", not "should we work with her?". About 17 sections carry equal weight, and the AI summary, the insights and the top metrics repeat each other.

Most numbers have no benchmark, and where one exists the label can mislead: 0.57 comments per 100 likes, against 1.62 for similar creators, is badged "Average". Add to list scrolls out of view, and the charts have no scales.

What I changed

I went deep on the three sections that decide the answer, and gave the rest a lighter pass.

  1. Answer first FocusThe first screen shows who she is (the AI persona), six key numbers with context, and a verdict: "Strong for awareness, light on conversation". Fit by campaign goal makes it concrete. Every strength and watch-out links to its evidence, and a confidence level sits underneath.
  2. Benchmarks with one rule FocusEvery metric sits on a shared scale against similar creators or her own average, with the same status colours everywhere. Reach is shown per follower, so a big account isn't mistaken for posts that travel far.
  3. Audience in two views of the same data Focus"At a glance" shows every breakdown in one scan. "100 followers" (dots or donut) explains the audience to a client. A target-match calculator answers "how much of her audience is mine?".
  4. Add to list never leaves the screenIt's in the header, the sticky bar and the mobile bar, with an L shortcut, multi-select lists and Undo. "Open on Instagram" stays alongside it.
  5. Missing data is designedGaps explain why they're empty and what to do next, and the verdict lowers its confidence.
  6. Motion that keeps contextCharts morph between tabs instead of redrawing: growth zooms between ranges, ranked bars slide into order, the donut resizes, and age bars split by gender. Motion is fast (under 0.7s) and turns off for reduced-motion users.

Supporting sections (Images vs Reels, ranked engagement, a compact sponsored vs organic check, content, brands, growth phases) follow the same rules.

Why

Marketers compare many creators in one sitting. Each change shortens the path from opening the page to a yes or no, and gives a reason to trust that answer.

Caveats

Figures come from the reference screenshot. These parts are sample data:

  • States, countries and follower history (and everything derived from it)
  • Most post thumbnails, and the gender split by age
  • AI tab copy beyond the summary

Goal fit and strengths are computed from benchmark thresholds; AI only writes the wording. It would need testing with real users and data.
